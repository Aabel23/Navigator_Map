## Q1. What gains did batched matching show over greedy nearest-driver, and what batch windows are typical?

### Takeaway
Uber, Lyft and Grab all moved from greedy "closest available driver" matching to batched bipartite assignment, which collects requests and drivers for a few seconds and solves the batch with the Hungarian method or a min-cost formulation. They describe the gains mostly qualitatively, as lower average wait across the network. No platform has published a clean "batch vs greedy" percentage for pickup ETA. Academic work shows the gains depend on the setting: batching helps a lot for pooling, helps little for single rides, and theory says it is not always better than greedy.

### Cited Findings
- Uber's official matching page: "In the seconds after a rider requests a ride, we evaluate nearby drivers and riders in one batch," pairing them "to reduce the average wait time for everyone, not just the closest pair." Uber says its earlier approach matched each rider to the closest available driver, which "worked well for most riders but sometimes led to long wait times for others." It also says a short delay lets a batch of potential matches accumulate, so collective wait is shorter. The page gives no numbers. — [Uber Marketplace: Matching](https://www.uber.com/us/en/marketplace/matching/)
- Lyft Engineering ("Solving Dispatch in a Ridesharing Problem Space") frames pairing as a linear program and solves each batch snapshot with the Hungarian method in polynomial time. It describes batch length as a trade-off: longer windows give better matches but delay assignment, shorter ones are faster but give weaker pairings. — [Lyft Eng blog](https://eng.lyft.com/solving-dispatch-in-a-ridesharing-problem-space-821d9606c3ff)
- Lyft's RL paper says Lyft runs batch matching "every few seconds" over unmatched requests and available drivers, using the Hungarian method. A worked toy example shows batch assignment cutting total rider wait plus pickup time by about 50% versus the alternative (greedy-like) assignment. This is an illustration, not a field result. — [Azagirre et al., arXiv 2310.13810](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13810)
- Grab: "Why Grab doesn't always assign the nearest driver." Its allocation AI weighs 40+ real-time factors, including ETA, driver trip preferences, fraud prevention, vehicle suitability, regional traffic laws and order batching, beyond pure distance. — [Grab Inside Grab](https://www.grab.com/inside-grab/stories/matching-driver-partners-and-riders-allocation/)
- Batch windows: academic papers describe batching as collecting requests for "a few seconds." One paper uses a 3-minute example window, typical for pooling and simulation studies. Third-party system-design write-ups cite 1–5 s windows and O(n³) Hungarian cost, but these are not platform sources. — [Bao et al. TR-C 2026 PDF](https://www.fransoliehoek.net/docs/Bao26TRC.pdf); [dev.to explainer (secondary)](https://dev.to/vesviet/system-design-ride-hailing-dispatch-algorithm-how-uber-disco-grab-dispatchgym-match-drivers-pp4)
- Adaptive vs fixed batch timing (Bao, Gao et al., TU Delft; arXiv 2503.13200 → Transportation Research Part C 2026): fixed-interval batching "cannot adapt to real-time supply-demand fluctuations and may induce unnecessary waiting." Deep RL that decides *when* to match cut total waiting time by 3.1% in ride-hailing and 20.1% in ride-pooling, against fixed-interval, rule-based and first-dispatch baselines. — [arXiv 2503.13200](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.13200); [TR-C 2026](https://www.fransoliehoek.net/docs/Bao26TRC.pdf)
- A dynamic pricing and matching study states batching can further reduce rider waiting time relative to the first-dispatch protocol. No percentage was surfaced. — [Dynamic pricing and matching in ride-hailing platforms (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/337282035_Dynamic_pricing_and_matching_in_ride-hailing_platforms)
- Theory is mixed. One result finds batching, like greedy, is asymptotically optimal if rider impatience is not too high, but batching requires more rider patience than greedy. Another finds batching can perform arbitrarily poorly for cost minimization and gives a greedy policy with a constant-factor guarantee. — [Batching and Optimal Multi-stage Bipartite Allocations, arXiv 2211.16581](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.16581) (and related results surfaced in search)
- Matching interval and matching radius are jointly tunable levers that affect wait and pickup distance. — [Yang et al., "Optimizing matching time interval and matching radius in on-demand ride-sourcing markets", Transportation Research Part B](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191261518311731)
- Wild-goose-chase effect: when prices are too low, an overloaded platform runs out of idle drivers and sends cars on long pickups. Uber data from Manhattan show this is a real problem. Under high load, matching quality depends on pricing as well as the matching algorithm. — [Castillo, Knoepfle (Uber), Weyl](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2017/MDf17/Castillo_Knoepfle_Weyl.pdf)
- Lyft (March 2026) studies non-exclusive dispatch, where several drivers are notified per request and First-Acceptance or Best-Acceptance contention decides who gets it. Welfare maximization is strongly NP-hard. The authors give a PTAS for single-rider First Acceptance and a (1−1/e) approximation for Best Acceptance (monotone submodular). Part II simulations report shorter match times and more completed matches than exclusive one-to-one dispatch, with a speed-vs-quality trade-off between the rules. — [arXiv 2603.21533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21533); [arXiv 2603.21531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21531)

### Inferences
- In practice the batch window runs from a couple of seconds to a few seconds for single rides. Platforms keep it short because wait added by batching counts directly against rider experience. Pooling tolerates longer windows, and that is where batching and adaptive timing show the largest measured gains (20.1% vs 3.1% in the TU Delft study).
- The main benefit of batching over greedy is avoiding bad "stealing" cases, where one rider's nearest driver would serve another rider much better. That matters most in dense markets with many simultaneous requests. In sparse markets a batch rarely holds more than one or two requests, so batching reduces to greedy.

### Gaps
- No primary platform source gives a measured pickup-ETA or completion-rate percentage for batch vs greedy. Uber and Grab pages are qualitative, and Lyft's 50% is a toy example.
- The specific batch window each platform uses (Uber/Lyft/Grab/DiDi) is not officially published. "2 seconds" is often cited for DiDi, but I could not verify it in a primary source this session.
- Uber "trip chaining" (matching a driver before drop-off): Uber help pages mention back-to-back trips and Trip Radar but do not describe the dispatch logic. USPTO "Ride chaining" patents describe tracking drivers about to drop off for matching, but search results did not confirm the assignee. — [Uber back-to-back trips page](https://www.uber.com/us/en/deliver/basics/making-deliveries/back-to-back-trips/); [Ride chaining patent 9679489](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9679489); [third-party blog describing trip chaining as used by Uber/Lyft](https://blog.afi.io/blog/using-route-optimization-to-build-a-ride-share-dispatch-algorithm/)

## Q2. DiDi's RL dispatch: papers, reported GMV and answer-rate gains, deployment details

### Takeaway
DiDi's line of work went from myopic combinatorial assignment, to learning-and-planning (KDD 2018: a tabular value function from historical data plus KM/bipartite assignment), to deep value networks (CVNet, KDD 2019), to multi-agent RL (KDD/WWW 2019–2020), to online RL "in the wild" (KDD 2022). Reported production gains are small in percentage terms but large in absolute value: about 0.5–5% GMV/completion (2018) and 1.3% driver income in A/B tests (2022), with up to 5.3% after full deployment by causal inference. The 2019 Wagner Prize recognized the program.

### Cited Findings
- **KDD 2018**, Xu, Li, et al. (incl. Jieping Ye), "Large-Scale Order Dispatch in On-Demand Ride-Hailing Platforms: A Learning and Planning Approach" (DOI 10.1145/3219819.3219824). Learning step: each vehicle-order pair is valued for immediate reward plus future gain, using spatiotemporal state values learned from historical data. Planning step: dispatch is solved with a combinatorial (bipartite) optimizer. — [ResearchGate entry](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/326495826_Large-Scale_Order_Dispatch_in_On-Demand_Ride-Hailing_Platforms_A_Learning_and_Planning_Approach); method described in [CoRide arXiv 1905.11353](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.11353)
  - Results, from secondary slide summaries: a two-week experiment across cities in China improved global GMV and completion rate by +0.5% to 5% versus the existing method. Average dispatch time did not change, so riders did not wait longer. A/B tests were split by time slices, not users, because users interfere with each other in a shared market. — [SlideShare KDD2018 DiDi summary](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/kdd2018-didi-largescale-order-dispatch-in-ondemand-ridehailing-platforms-a-learning-and-planning-approach/160184818); [Speaker Deck (pacocat)](https://speakerdeck.com/pacocat/takusipei-che-arugorizumuhefalseqiang-hua-xue-xi-huo-yong-reinforcement-learning-applications-in-taxi-dispatching-and-repositioning-domain)
- **ICDM 2018**, Wang, Qin, Tang, Ye, Zhu, "Deep Reinforcement Learning with Knowledge Transfer for Online Rides Order Dispatching." MDP formulation using DQN with action search, plus transfer learning across cities. It is sometimes wrongly cited as KDD 2019. — [IEEE Xplore 8594886](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8594886/)
- **KDD 2019 (CVNet)**, Tang, Qin, Zhang, Wang, Xu, Ma, Zhu, Ye, "A Deep Value-network Based Approach for Multi-Driver Order Dispatching," pp. 1780–1790. A deep, cerebellar-embedded value network replaces the tabular value. The planning step is still bipartite assignment. — [ACM DL 10.1145/3292500.3330724](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3292500.3330724)
- **Interfaces / INFORMS J. Applied Analytics 50(5):272–286 (2020)**, Qin, Tang, et al., "Ride-Hailing Order Dispatching at DiDi via Reinforcement Learning." Winner of the 2019 INFORMS Wagner Prize. It traces the evolution from myopic combinatorial optimization to a semi-MDP model with deep RL for long-term optimization. — [INFORMS Wagner video page](https://www.informs.org/Resource-Center/Video-Library/Wagner-Competition-Videos/2019-Wagner-Competition-Videos/2019-Wagner-Prize-Winner-Ride-hailing-Order-Dispatching-on-DiDi-via-Reinforcement-Learning); [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/orinte/v50y2020i5p272-286.html)
- **Multi-agent RL**: CoRide (CIKM 2019) does joint order dispatching and fleet management. Li et al. (WWW 2019) do MARL via order-vehicle distribution matching. — [CoRide arXiv 1905.11353](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.11353); [MARL order-vehicle distribution matching](https://deepai.org/publication/multi-agent-reinforcement-learning-for-order-dispatching-via-order-vehicle-distribution-matching)
- **KDD 2021**, "Value Function is All You Need: A Unified Learning Framework for Ride Hailing Platforms" (Tang et al.). Reported to beat the KDD Cup 2020 RL-track winner (PolarB) and CVNet on total driver income. Large online A/B tests on DiDi showed significant improvement in driver income and user-experience metrics. — [arXiv 2105.08791](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.08791v3); [review](https://liner.com/review/value-function-is-all-you-need-unified-learning-framework-for)
- **KDD 2022**, "Reinforcement Learning in the Wild: Scalable RL Dispatching Algorithm Deployed in Ridehailing Marketplace" (RLW). Values a driver's destination by expected future earnings and accounts for cancellation/rejection probability. Uses adaptive graph pruning based on multi-armed bandits to reduce post-assignment cancellation. Deployed for A/B tests in multiple DiDi cities and launched as the primary dispatch mode in a major international market. Results: >1.3% improvement in total driver income in A/B tests (a summary also reports +0.422% completion rate and +0.296% answer rate), and up to 5.3% improvement in major metrics after full-scale deployment by causal inference. The authors note time-flipping A/B designs plus non-stationarity give conservative estimates. — [arXiv 2202.05118](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05118); [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3534678.3539095)
- DiDi open-sourced its simulation environment for the KDD Cup 2020 RL track (dispatch + repositioning). — [Real-world Vehicle Repositioning arXiv 2103.04555](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.04555v3)

### Inferences
- Across DiDi's papers the stable architecture is: learn a value function V(location, time[, context]) offline or online, set each edge weight to immediate reward + γ^Δt·V(dest) − V(origin), then solve a bipartite assignment (KM/Hungarian) per batch. RL changes the edge weights; the per-batch solver stays the same.
- Production gains of about 0.5–5% look small, but at DiDi/Lyft scale they mean tens of millions of USD per year. For a small platform the same percentage would likely be lost in noise.

### Gaps
- I could not read the exact KDD 2018 A/B table (which metric reached 5% in which city) or CVNet's online numbers. The full texts could not be fetched.
- I found no DiDi dispatch publication from 2023–2026 with production numbers.

## Q3. Uber, Lyft and Grab public writeups on dispatch, surge pricing and forecasting, including 2023–2026

### Takeaway
Lyft has the best-documented production RL matcher: online-learned driver value functions on top of Hungarian batch matching, deployed globally in 2021, worth more than $30M/yr, and an Edelman finalist. It also published non-exclusive dispatch work in 2026. Uber's public material covers batched matching (qualitative), DeepETA/DeeprETA (ETA residual learning), LSTM extreme-event forecasting, and the economics of surge pricing. Grab released DispatchGym (2025), an RL research framework, rather than production numbers.

### Cited Findings
- **Lyft RL matching** (Azagirre et al., INFORMS J. Applied Analytics 54(1):71–83, 2024; arXiv 2310.13810): online RL estimates drivers' expected future earnings (driver value function V(s) = expected discounted long-term reward) in real time and feeds them into matching. Billed as the first documented ridesharing matcher that learns and improves in real time. Deployed globally in 2021. Switchback experiments in most markets estimate >$30M/yr incremental revenue, with drivers serving millions of additional riders per year. A co-author's profile lists it as a 2023 Franz Edelman finalist. — [arXiv 2310.13810](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13810); [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/inte.2023.0083)
- **Lyft non-exclusive notifications (2026)**: see Q1. — [arXiv 2603.21533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21533); [arXiv 2603.21531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21531)
- **Lyft map-matching**: noisy GPS feeds both ETA estimates and dispatch, so map-matching quality directly affects dispatch quality. — [Lyft Eng: real-time map-matching](https://eng.lyft.com/a-new-real-time-map-matching-algorithm-at-lyft-da593ab7b006)
- **Uber DeepETA / DeeprETA**: a routing engine computes a base ETA as the sum of segment traversal times, and an ML model predicts the residual between that ETA and actual outcomes. The paper describes a deep residual network with discretized/embedded features and a linear-attention, transformer-like encoder, replacing earlier gradient-boosted tree ensembles. Offline and online tests show significant MAE improvements. No public production % was found. — [Uber blog: DeepETA](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/deepeta-how-uber-predicts-arrival-times/); [DeeprETA arXiv 2206.02127](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.02127)
- **Uber demand forecasting**: a 2017 blog and ICML 2017 workshop paper (Laptev, Yosinski, Li, Smyl). One LSTM is trained across many cities for extreme events (holidays, concerts, weather), with exogenous weather and city features. Trained offline in TensorFlow/Keras, with weights exported to native Go. A follow-up adds Bayesian NN uncertainty estimation. — [Uber blog: extreme event forecasting](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/neural-networks/); [Uber blog: uncertainty estimation](https://www.uber.com/kw/en/blog/neural-networks-uncertainty-estimation/)
- **Surge pricing economics**:
  - Castillo, Knoepfle (Uber), Weyl: surge pricing prevents the "wild goose chase," where underpricing exhausts idle drivers and forces very long pickups. Evidence comes from Uber Manhattan data. — [NBER PDF](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2017/MDf17/Castillo_Knoepfle_Weyl.pdf)
  - Castillo, "Who Benefits from Surge Pricing?", Econometrica 2025 (Houston Uber data): surge raises rider surplus by 3.57% of gross revenue and lowers driver surplus by 0.98% and current platform profit by 0.50% of gross revenue. Drivers who work long hours are hurt most. — [Econometrica](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.3982/ECTA19106); [JMP PDF](https://economics.sas.upenn.edu/system/files/2020-01/JMP_Castillo.pdf)
- **Grab DispatchGym** (engineering blog, 7 July 2025): an open-source Python RL research framework for Grab's dispatch, which matches bookings with drivers. It combines an RL algorithm module, a dispatch-process simulator (Numba-accelerated), and a Gymnasium API, with experiments run via CLI on Spark. It has been used to prototype contextual bandits (linear, neural-linear, GP) with ε-greedy and Thompson sampling to tune dispatch hyperparameters. The post notes RL is hard here because of reward design and action levers. For example, a limit on drivers considered per booking gives little benefit beyond a small number. — [Grab Engineering: DispatchGym](https://engineering.grab.com/techblog_-dispatchgym)
- **Grab batching for deliveries**: when delivery partners are scarce, Grab batches food orders to meet promised windows, considering item type, weight and prep time, then routes the pickup and drop-off sequence. — [Grab: nearest driver](https://www.grab.com/inside-grab/stories/matching-driver-partners-and-riders-allocation/); [Grab productivity story](https://www.grab.com/inside-grab/stories/driver-earnings-productivity-efficiency-tech-tools/)
- **RideGym (2026)**: an arXiv paper proposing a standardized interface for real-world large-scale ride-sharing simulation. — [arXiv 2607.10173](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.10173)

### Inferences
- The industry has converged on a common pattern: batched bipartite assignment, with edge weights from learned ETA plus learned long-term value. Lyft (online TD) and DiDi (offline/online value nets) both document it, and Uber's public material is consistent with it.
- Surge pricing is a matching-efficiency tool, not only a revenue tool. Without it, high load degrades matching through long pickups.

- I found no Lyft public writeups on surge/pricing ML or driver repositioning (the Lyft-positioning search returned nothing relevant).
- Hall, Kendrick & Nosko's Uber surge study did not appear in search results, so it is not cited.

## Q4. Supply-demand forecasting, repositioning and pooled rides

### Takeaway
Repositioning (moving idle drivers toward predicted demand) and pooling both build on the same machinery. Field evidence for RL repositioning is modest: DiDi's 1,200-driver pilot did not reach statistical significance. Pooling has the strongest simulation evidence: a fleet of about 15–25% of NYC taxis could serve about 98–99% of demand with about 2.5–3 min waits.

### Cited Findings
- **DiDi repositioning** ("Real-world Ride-hailing Vehicle Repositioning using Deep Reinforcement Learning," TR-C 2021 / arXiv 2103.04555): deep RL plus decision-time planning, compared against experienced drivers repositioning on their own judgment. Field pilot with about 1,200 drivers in 3 cities via an AI driver-assistant app. Results were positive, but differences in income per hour and utilization versus control were statistically insignificant. — [arXiv 2103.04555](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.04555v3)
- DiDi driver-centric DQN/PPO for joint dispatch and repositioning beat all baselines by >6% in simulation and is reportedly deployed in production. — [ResearchGate: Multi-Driver Dispatching and Repositioning](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/337560403_Deep_Reinforcement_Learning_for_Multi-Driver_Vehicle_Dispatching_and_Repositioning_Problem)
- MARL reward design for driver repositioning. — [arXiv 2002.06723](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.06723)
- **Pooling** (Alonso-Mora et al., PNAS 2017, NYC ~3M taxi requests): 2,000 vehicles (15% of the taxi fleet) with capacity 10, or 3,000 with capacity 4, serve 98% of demand with a mean wait of 2.8 min and a mean delay of 3.5 min. 3,000 vehicles with capacity 2 or 4 serve 94% or 98%. The Results section states that a fleet under 25% of active taxis can serve 99% of requests with about 2.5 min wait and delay. Rebalancing idle vehicles raised the service rate by about 20%. Method: a shareability graph, then a request-trip-vehicle (RTV) graph, then an ILP per batch (30 s batches in the paper). A correction was issued in January 2018 (scope unclear). — [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1611675114); [supplement](https://people.csail.mit.edu/jalonsom/docs/17-alonsomora-ridesharing-pnas-supplemental.pdf); [unofficial implementation](https://github.com/MetaZuo/RideSharing)
- Adaptive match timing gives a 20.1% wait reduction for pooling vs 3.1% for single rides (see Q1). — [arXiv 2503.13200](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.13200)
- Predictive repositioning for ride-pooling. — [arXiv 2308.05507](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.05507)
- Forecasting: see Uber's LSTM and BNN work in Q3. — [Uber blog](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/neural-networks/)

### Inferences
- For a small project, heat-map or "suggested zone" repositioning driven by simple per-cell demand forecasts (gradient boosting or seasonal baselines per H3 cell and time slot) is more realistic than RL. DiDi's own pilot shows RL repositioning has hard-to-measure gains.
- Pooling requires per-batch solving of a combinatorially larger problem (RTV + ILP) and longer batch windows (tens of seconds). It is a phase-2 feature.

### Gaps
- The "30 s batch" for Alonso-Mora comes from my prior knowledge of the paper; search snippets did not confirm it.
- I found no public production metrics for UberPool/Lyft Shared match rates.

## Q5. Which approach fits which scale, and which open-source tools a small project can use

### Takeaway
Start with greedy nearest-by-ETA, or batched assignment with `scipy.optimize.linear_sum_assignment` on a sparse candidate set (k nearest drivers within radius R) every 2–5 s. Move to OR-Tools min-cost flow or sparse LAP solvers as volume grows. Add learned ETA and then learned value functions only once there is enough volume and experiment infrastructure (switchback A/B tests) to measure 1–5% effects. Building the cost matrix (road-network ETAs) usually costs more than the assignment solve.

### Cited Findings
- SciPy `linear_sum_assignment` (dense LSAP, roughly O(n^2.3) in practice per a SciPy-dev discussion) is adequate for a few thousand nodes. Memory is the constraint: a 30,000×30,000 float64 matrix is about 6.7 GB, and SciPy's internal copy can double that. — [nanolsap PyPI/GitHub](https://github.com/hzqmwne/nanolsap); [SciPy-dev 2020](https://www.python.org/pipermail/scipy-dev/2020-July/024268.html)
- lapsolver reports solving 5000×5000 in about 3 s. A 2026 neural warm-start paper reports about 2× speedup over SciPy at N=16,384 (authors' own benchmark). — [lapsolver](https://libraries.io/pypi/lapsolver); [arXiv 2605.09382](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.09382)
- Ride matching can be formulated as min-cost flow, including joint pricing and matching on NYC taxi data solved in practical time. OR-Tools `SimpleMinCostFlow` provides the API (arcs with capacity and unit cost). — [arXiv 2404.19241](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.19241)
- Edge-cost computation (shortest-path travel times on the road network) often dominates runtime in matching pipelines. — [VLDB vol.14 p1150 (Abeywickrama et al.)](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol14/p1150-abeywickrama.pdf)
- RL dispatch research tooling: Grab DispatchGym (open-source, Gymnasium API) and DiDi's KDD Cup 2020 simulation environment. — [Grab DispatchGym](https://engineering.grab.com/techblog_-dispatchgym); [arXiv 2103.04555](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.04555v3)
- Pooling reference implementation (unofficial Alonso-Mora RTV). — [GitHub MetaZuo/RideSharing](https://github.com/MetaZuo/RideSharing)
- Experiment design: dispatch changes need time-sliced or switchback A/B tests, not user-randomized tests, because riders and drivers interfere. DiDi used time-slicing, Lyft used switchbacks. — [DiDi KDD2018 slides](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/kdd2018-didi-largescale-order-dispatch-in-ondemand-ridehailing-platforms-a-learning-and-planning-approach/160184818); [Lyft arXiv 2310.13810](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13810)

### Inferences
- **Tier 0 (MVP, <~50 concurrent requests per city)**: greedy by road ETA, or broadcast to the k nearest drivers with first-accept. Lyft's 2026 work shows non-exclusive notification is a legitimate design with known trade-offs. Batching adds little when batches are tiny.
- **Tier 1 (hundreds to low thousands of concurrent requests/drivers per city)**: batch every 2–5 s. Prune candidates to the top-k drivers by ETA within a radius. Solve with `linear_sum_assignment` (pad with dummy "unmatched" cost) or OR-Tools min-cost flow (sparse). Partition by city or geo-cell to keep each problem small. Cost = pickup ETA plus penalties (cancel risk, driver acceptance probability).
- **Tier 2 (large platform)**: add learned ETA (DeepETA-style residual on a routing engine), surge pricing, demand forecasts per cell, and a value-function term in edge weights (DiDi/Lyft style). Gains of about 1–5% are only measurable with switchback infrastructure and large volume.
- Latency budget: per-batch solve plus ETA computation must fit well inside the batch window. At Tier 1 scale this is easily sub-second with sparse candidates. ETA lookups (OSRM/Valhalla/H3 precomputed tables) are the main cost.

### Gaps
- I found no published per-batch latency budgets (ms) from Uber, Lyft, Grab or DiDi.
- I found no head-to-head benchmark of OR-Tools min-cost flow vs SciPy on ride-matching workloads.
